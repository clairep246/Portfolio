Building Magnetic through NUS Orbital

Orbital is a summer programme by NUS where students work in teams to develop a product from the ground up. It gave me the opportunity to apply what I had learnt in school while exploring an area that I had little experience in. For my Orbital project, I worked on Magnetic, a platform designed to help students make friends and build meaningful connections with one another.

The idea for Magnetic came from the difficulty some students face when trying to make friends, especially when entering a new environment. It can be challenging to meet people with similar interests, so we wanted to create a platform where students could discover and connect with others based on shared interests and experiences.

Learning web development from scratch

This project was an eye-opening experience for me because I had no prior experience in web development. At the beginning, even simple tasks felt unfamiliar, and I often had to spend a lot of time understanding concepts that I had never encountered before.

I had to learn many things from scratch while trying to apply them directly to our project. Although this was challenging, I gradually became more comfortable with the development process and picked up many new skills along the way.

Learning to solve problems independently

One of the most important things I learnt was how to approach problems independently. Throughout the development process, we encountered many bugs and problems that we did not immediately know how to solve.

One example was linking input data to the Gemini model using Supabase Edge Functions. It took me almost a week to experiment with different solutions and figure out why the requests were being blocked on the server side. Instead of relying on someone else to give me the answer, I had to understand error messages, identify where the problem might be coming from, search for relevant information, and test different solutions.

Although it was frustrating at times, finally solving the problem made me more confident in my ability to handle unfamiliar issues.

Iteration and product building

Orbital showed me that I do not need to know everything before starting something new. Being willing to experiment, make mistakes, and learn from them can be just as important as having existing technical knowledge.

I also learnt the importance of iteration. Our initial ideas did not always work exactly as we expected. As we developed Magnetic, we had to reconsider certain features, fix issues, and make changes based on what we discovered. This helped me understand that building a product is not a straightforward process where everything follows the original plan.

Most importantly, Orbital gave me a better understanding of what it takes to turn an idea into a working product. Having an idea is very different from actually implementing it. We had to think about the user experience, write the code, debug problems, and ensure that the different parts of the platform worked together.

Looking back

Overall, Orbital was a valuable learning experience for me. Although the learning curve was initially steep, I gained much more than just technical knowledge. I became more comfortable with unfamiliar technologies, improved my problem-solving skills, and learnt how to work through setbacks.

Magnetic started as an idea, but the process of turning it into a working platform taught me a lot about software development and about myself as a learner.
